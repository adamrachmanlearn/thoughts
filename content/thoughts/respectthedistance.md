Despite a decade of running, my personal bests are.. underwhelming to say the least.

<br>

Just this morning, I finally set a new number to my longest run, that is 12k (lol). My previous is around 11k, it was also super pathetic, because it was a failed attempt of a 15k, got forced to stop because of knee pain, then can't run for around 3 months. It was the worst.

<br>

Honestly speaking, I'm grateful enough that the injury could *heal* by themselves even if it took 3 months.

<br>

#### Respecting the distance

<br>

Now, one of many things that made me do it was, on the internet, we often see incredible results day in and day out, It seems that *everybody* doing it like it is the norm.

<br>

I know that, in a way, this is a good motivator, but often time, we forgot that to get to that point, they put a lot of invisible effort behind the posts, this made us often sneer at the *shorter* distance, approaching it recklessly, cause honestly, preparing ourselves completely for a 5k seems very *uncool*.

<br>

After all of those *lessons* though, current me think to always respect the distance no matter how *unimpressive* it sounds, even if it's only 10k, 5k, 1k, heck, even 800 and 400 deserves one.

<br>

By respecting the distance, you would approach your training carefully, you'll consider all possibility, that alone will push your ego down, and hopefully, prevent unnecessary injury.

<br>

A good runner isn't the fastest, it's the one who can sticks to it in a long time.
