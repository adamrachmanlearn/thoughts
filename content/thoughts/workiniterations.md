I unironically *hate* this characteristic of mine: [perfectionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfectionism_(psychology)), now.. no, this isn’t a humblebrag, in fact it’s far from it.

<br>

Perfectionism actively screws my life (lol). As you’ve might read from my [other post](#the-actual-reason-you-do-things), being praised as a kid made me strive for perfection, or at least, a very high degree of it, later in life it turns into fear, fear of coming short of something, fear of producing half-assed value.

<br>

Because of that, when I want to do stuffs, I usually kept it for myself for a long time, I try to look-ahead of many problems that could possibly affect the result, problem-solves a problem that doesn’t exist yet, literally I become that [one scene](https://wegotthiscovered.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Screen-Shot-2022-05-02-at-13.26.50-PM-e1652147104847.jpg) from Doctor Strange.

<br>

#### An antidote.. maybe

<br>

*What if* I can work on something without worrying the far future, *what if* I can get multiple chances to work on something, reducing the possibility of messing it up, *if only* there’s a way.. Well it actually.. exist?!

<br>

#### Enter, iterations!

<br>

I was on my final year of college, it’s the usual *conduct your research for your final assignment*-time, long story short I did mine in web development, the method I used was [Scrum](https://www.scrum.org/resources/what-scrum-module). To put it very simply, that method emphasized producing an *acceptable* product as fast as possible, yes.. *acceptable*, not decent, not good, and certainly *far* from perfect.

<br>

As much as it weirded me out, it was also.. liberating, now you see.. there’s another element to it, it’s the-*as fast as possible*, the reason it’s there is actually so we can move forward and do another iterations. Then another, then another, then another.

<br>

This repeated action of patching, improving, optimizing, was the secret sauce to a great results. It might be obvious for most people, but I don’t know why, I was blind to this, it’s almost like, I want to do these iterations in my head, then suddenly perfect result out of thin air, call it prideful, arrogant, or just.. plain stupid, I don’t even know.

<br>

*Maybe I’m just scared*.

<br>

What if those people who praised me, see and think, *“not so bright these days, eh?”*, what if my identity as a smart person shattered, I can cope however i want, but deep down, maybe I care too much, maybe the identity was literally all to my name, I was scared of losing the badge.

<br>

#### The practice

<br>

From that moment, I practiced to do just that, to be able to produce subpar result at a decent speed, then do the things all over again, 2, 4, 6, or however many it takes. Sure there was days where I couldn’t muster enough courage to do the *first iteration*, those days I procrastinate like crazy, but I also grateful that there’s decent amount of days where I actually pull through.

<br>

Everything worthwhile in this world was built upon hundreds and thousands of iterations.

<br>

You might read this after the sun sets, so what? No problem! With my lamp, I can read, study, play, anything, at night. The guy that invented them? Yeah, the [Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison) guy, apparently he went through so much [failure](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/7-epic-fails-brought-to-you-by-the-genius-mind-of-thomas-edison-180947786/), it’s not even funny.

<br>

Next step was not grieving, he just said okay cool, noted, we *move on* to the next iteration.
