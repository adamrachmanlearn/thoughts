If you've read past couple of posts, you might think that *"this guys whines a lot"*, and you're right, what can I say, I've been exhausted these days, but today, I wanna talk about something else, a bit more *positive* if you could say so.

<br>

I hate studying, at least for the most part, it began around the time I was enrolled in junior high, a *gifted-kid-title-holder* me was not able to accept the harsh facts that I, am in fact, not so gifted. Studying became one of these things that would exposed me, I hate the feeling of not being able to understand right away, it aggravated by the fact that other people did.

<br>

I ended up in this weird spot of doing bare minimum so that I could pass the *test*, yet not giving my all, hence I'll never be *founded out*, now that I think about it, doing this would ended up giving me the worst of both worlds (lol), It gives me the feeling that I actually tried, yet because of my subpar effort, I get very little, so it kept me in this cycle of *I-tried-yet-I-can't-seem-to-get-it*, and don't get me started on how frustrating it felt.

<br>

Recently though, I think I lucked out and found a glitch on this thing. To give you a bit of context, if you could remove all the bad things about me (which you need a lot of miracles lmao), I honestly am a very curious kid, not that grand, certainly not something that could be told to media about some successful person, no, it's much more modest.

<br>

Think of it as, a nosy person who thinks they need to know everything, like I remember when I was a kid, I drank one of those probiotics beverages, the thing had frequent advertisement on TV, something along the line of *"..good bacteria.."*, while others would just drank it just because it tasted good (of course, it has like tons of sugar lol), me, on the other hand, was kept bugging my mother with questions about how a bacteria could be good? On is it really *bacteria* that I've drank? Or as silly as trying to *see* the thing by eye-balling the plastic packaging ~1cm into my pupil.

<br>

And while lots of that curiousities been buried under, I can't help but notice that they pop out from time to time. Now where do I want to go with these? Well, I notice that when I *learned* things purely (or mostly) out of curiousity, I tend to fare better than when I was *forced*, far better.

<br>

It used to be hard to notice because back when I was on school or college, they took huge amount of my time, so that majority of things I'd do was to fulfill the obligation of either of those two, think about it like assignments, essays, exams, tests, etc, you got the point, so yeah, there's practically zero curiosity from my end to act as a *fuel* for me, the whole journey was so hard.

<br>

After I graduated, though there's still some obligation (there'll always be, sadly), I found myself with a bit more free time, and in those times, without even having to schedule anything, this buried curiousity of mine started to take control autonomously, I started to browsed through various of things, one of them being this very *blog* if you would say so.

<br>

If I could be so honest with you, from the perspective of someone who coded before (not even professional, students who aspires to be one, heck, even a casual hobby-ist), this site of mine is laughably simple, even more with the help of generative AI these days, you could built something like this in less than hour, like to be honest.

<br>

But for me, it went deeper than that. The curiousity, that led me to this, resulting in me having to spent hours if not days in a code editor, repeatedly thinking of quitting this, broke a function or two, learning the basics of git and GitHub, etc. Now, I have a bachelor in computer stuffs, and these things should be menial for me, yet this one felt genuinely good, because I felt like I could be passionate in something (again), I felt like I genuinely studied the thing, I felt like I could muster almost maximum effort. I felt like I, actually growing.

<br>

And all of these could happened because I was on pure curiousity as a fuel, no obligations to ace that test, to pass the exams, or to write impressive essays, there's no obligations to.. perform, I could drop the *gifted-kid* title, comfortably even.

<br>

And d\*mn do that felt good. Of course theres bunch of things I intended to be *curiosity-based* project that *failed*, but these things gave me hope that, there's still chance that I could pick up something from out there, and It genuinely could provoke the curious kid in me, letting myself to be free to look stupid and enjoy the process.
