As of writing this, I just did a 4k in 44 minutes, that meant my *pace* was averaging around 10:00/km.

<br>

You don’t need to be engrossed in running to know that was *freakishly slow* (lol), but that was also one of my best run ever. In this post I want to share about it mostly from my experience without touching much of the science behind it.

<br>

#### My own world

<br>

Here’s the big opener. If you guys are similar with me, that is you have an annoyingly noisy head, I’d recommend you to do it, also this might be a preference, but for me it *must* be without music.

<br>

Assuming you ran slow enough (might be slower than you would expect), after a couple a minutes, you should feel that there’s *something* that *envelopes* you, the surroundings felt duller and duller, until you arrived at this state of *serenity*, treat yourself and try to maintain it for at least 10 minutes.

<br>

> &#9888; For safety reasons, you could try it in a loop *far* from main road first.

<br>

#### Other option to change your scene

<br>

Sometimes we just can’t bear the monotony of daily live, some people go to their favorite cafe, buy that new decorations, etc. For me a minimum of 15 minutes of slow running is often the best, bonus point if I can run that new route to kinda explore, or even the same ol’ route often changed and we don’t notice cause our usual *pace of life*.

<br>

Aside from increasing your steps count for the day and burn some of those excess calories, it’s also *free* and have little to no preparation, which is the best part, honestly.

<br>

#### Overall feeling to the body

<br>

Many of us have ill view towards running, most of it came from our experience in PE class, where we’re told to run our lungs out (lol). Fun fact! Did you know that you can, *not* do that (lol). Running at slower pace don’t occupy your lungs, your vision isn’t get blurry, then you can run for longer, further.

<br>

The feeling persisted until you finish the run, even to the next couple of days, cause we dont exert our body too much, there’s fewer work to repair it. It’s also why I can run almost everyday, not because I’m strong, it’s just that I adjust the intensity.

<br>

Sure max or sub-max effort running isn’t all bad, in fact it has it’s own [merits](https://www.primalplay.com/blog/7-benefits-of-sprinting) and places. Me myself still tries to pick up my pace here and there, it’s just a subtle reminder that, for these kinds of activity, sometimes just prioritize what you enjoy, rather than following the masses.

<br>

If you do run, feel how your body felt.
If you do trail, get a load of your surroundings.
If you cycle, embrace the gentle wind on your face.

<br>

Enjoyment *could* arises not from the highs of metrics, but from the lows of expectation.
