It's been crazy these days, bad news practically everyday, things went south really fast, the weather been getting hotter and hotter, and finally, those damn mosquitoes *jumped* me nights after nights, goodbye quality sleep!

<br>

These days, I'm practically living on edge, one inconvenience away from snapping out, super stressed. My running program took a toll too, obviously. I recovered way longer than I used to, the *niggles* lingers around much longer, even there's days when I practically have difficulties with just walking.

<br>

I've learned something though, in those days where I walk like an elderly, I felt good, I guess this is one of those theory where mind and body are connected, once I slow down physically, my mind seems to follows it, these analog signals from body seems to be the highest priorities for the brain to process.

<br>

I guess it's obvious, yeah, like how you're supposed to calm down, trying bunch of breathing exercise or remembering those stoic quote you read from random article online, when youre practically power walking, upstairs, chasing that bus that will depart like right now?

<br>

We often moves fast, too fast even, I get it, we all trying to reach a place, ideally not 20 minutes late, and in this urban hell hole, with all of the traffics (if you live in rural area, enjoy it), it's lowkey a tall order.

<br>

But trust me, after some time observing these super rushed people, I often (not always, but often) bumps again with them further along the route, and this is true for both when you rode your own vehicles or using the public transportation, things like unlucky red lights, random vehicles on wrong spots, some road-rage occurrences, you name it. It's not even accounting for the worst case of you crashing or fall from that stairs.

<br>

Sure you might save that 20 minutes, but the risk just not worth to take, and you can save some time in a safer way, such as just simply depart earlier (sure it isn't viable in some case, but you got the point).

<br>

Also, when I accept that I'll maybe late here and there when I can't walk fast, turns out, on average, I arrived within the same periods as when I rushed like my life depends on it, apparently it gives me nothing more than panic attacks and drenched shirts from sweat, yuck.

<br>

So nowadays, I'm constantly trying to walk and move slower, sometimes it isn't easy to do, but I felt much better If I do. You could try, at first, in order to not triggers your anxiety, just depart earlier, even 10 minutes earlier can give you the mental headspace to *validate* your thought, wanting to walk slower, don't look at watch helps too.

<br>

I think you'll surprised when the time difference isn't that big, whereas the difference it made in your peace of mind is THAT significant.
