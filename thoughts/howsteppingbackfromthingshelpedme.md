Did you ever find yourself in a situation where you know you’ve been tinkering with something for *far too long*? For me, it’s like weekly occurence.

With my [perfectionism](#work-in-iterations) tendency, it’s so easy to fall into this, one time I even wiped my operating system multiple times a day just because I felt that I was *clicking a button wrong*, like how the hell did you even press a button wrong?

Okay, my example might be too extreme (or not, then I emphatized with you, my friend), but I assume you have something similar to a certain degree: working on that document, finishing an assignment, creating your next painting, tweaking your gaming system’s RGB setup, the list goes on and on, you get the gist.

The thing is, after a while this can burn us out, making us doubt ourselves, *“maybe this is not for me after all”*. God forbid it comes from our mouth. Now let me share my point of view on this, I hope this give you the courage to keep at it somehow.

#### The danger of expectation

Why we feel discouraged? My assumption is simple, when we start doing something, we put some kind of expectation in the back of our mind, we want things to go well, I mean, it’s reasonable, who wouldn’t. But sometimes this expectation pressuring us, even when we don’t notice.

Now here’s my anecdote.

Couple of months back, I gather a bit of money to bought a new bicycle, sure, it isn’t cheap and was a decent financial decision to made, but I was quite confident in it, because: A. I love exercise in general, B. I was someone who cycles everywhere from elementary to high school period (I was the minority, because my peers usually transitioned to motorcycle as soon as late-elementary to early-junior high school period).

*What could go wrong? Am I right?*, boy oh boy.. Instead of re-living the imagined nostalgia, I was damned in like, one week in. Here’s what happened, with the new bicycle, also comes new knowledge, I start dabbling in those bicycle forums and all.

Fast forward, instead of gleefully riding it, I was busy tweaking it nonstop. It actually got so bad, I was buying multiple bike parts *week in* and *week out*, tried to find a sweet spot between ergonomic position without making it look *goofy*.

I was tired.. like really tired, it really discouraged me because this thing, who I imagined supposed to improve my overall happiness, turned out into something that would only gather dust in the garage.

#### Stepping back was the answer

After that mental (and physical) wrestles, I sadly need to accept it, I leaned the thing to a wall then I *stepped back*.

Life goes on, and while it’s a heartbreak story, it also made me appreciate running more and more, I was so grateful that the same fate didn’t hit it like it hits cycling. Weeks past by while slowly erasing the sentimental feeling towards the bicycle, until today..

Today is a rest day in my running schedule, it’s as if *the right time* actually came. I accidentally see the thing and thought, *“maybe I can tweak it here, here, and here, maybe then, it’ll become decent”*, and so I did! I test it a bit, and now while writing this, lo and behold, I’ve done a decent 10km ride on it, with stupidly wide grin on my face through the ride nonetheless, I was very happy.

Maybe the *thing* we need isn’t to *engage more*, it’s actually to *give it some space*.

As it happened to me, I hope the same thing happens to you too. If you’re currently working on something that felt so desperately difficult, take a break, and it’s not like you need weeks like I did (though you could try), even a simple *day-off* will helps to primes your mind the next time you go at it.

*Stepping back* isn’t killing your momentum, it brought new perspective inside a clear mind.
