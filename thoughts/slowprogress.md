As of writing this, I've finally passed the 30km mark on my weekly running mileage.

<br>

How I achieved it isn't pretty though, had to practically drag my ass through it, but hey, at least one of my goals checked off the list right?

<br>

Anyway, it's been like two days into my next block, which fortunately is a *deload* week, it simply means that I allowed to run less mileage this week, but for the last two days I've been a bit discouraged, because I've been hoping, with this less mileage I can run faster right? Nope, it looks like my legs were barely able to pass last week, it's still hurting pretty much everytime I tried to do something with it.

<br>

So now I'm settling with lower mileage **and** slower pace too, don't get me wrong, [I like to run slow](#why-my-best-runs-are-often-the-slowest), it's just one of those *runner's high* after achieving something, that made you just want to go full throttle lol.

<br>

It reminds me of something though, often times, progress is built on a *painfully* slow rate, me myself often grow impatient with everything, internet makes an illusion, that progress should come at an absurdly instant speed, while most of them (arguably healthier way) should come with time.

<br>

While the realization seeps in, I tried to reduce the intensity, and yeah, while my legs still feels somewhat awful, it felt better and better, this is good, I'd prefer to stick with this for a long time to come, not for achieving a single feat then go back to where I began.

<br>

From my experience, something that built slowly, should also last for a very long time, and vice versa.

<br>

So, maybe if you're doing something and it felt painfully slow, just kept at it, you're doing just fine.
